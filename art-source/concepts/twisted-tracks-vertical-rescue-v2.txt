Crossing review and design requirements
1. Southeast, beside the blue train: restore red rails and debris beneath the
   later sandy shortcut. A supported timber footbridge crosses OVER those rails
   from the southern perimeter to the eastern landing. Dirt ends at the landing;
   planks, cross beams, posts and shadows explain the crossing's height.
2. Central southern entrance: make a short supported plank crossing over the
   low rail, instead of painting earth through a steel tube.
3. Central lower passage: keep it on exposed ground, beneath a braced yellow
   support with real headroom. Any intersecting low rail needs a plank crossing
   or a real opening. The foreground beam hides the passing actor correctly.
4. Upper catwalk: route arrows must rest on completed physical decking. An
   unfinished gap blocks its route until its construction job is complete.
5. Train-side platform: connect its exit to the southeast bridge. Rescue pockets
   cannot be isolated decorations. Ladders need upper and lower landings.
6. Perimeter and western dirt paths: keep earth where it follows exposed ground.
   A bridge is needed only where an actual rail/debris obstruction is crossed.

Construction kit mechanic: proposed first prototype
One clickable task family with several authored results. At each missing link,
place a box of parts on the reachable side: brackets, rope, timber or ladder
sections. Clicking assigns an ordinary worker to assemble that location's
predefined ladder, bridge or brace. There is no construction choice menu.

Unbuilt: crate + visibly missing/blocked connection; no path beyond it.
Queued/working: use the ordinary task queue, worker and resource requirements.
Built: crate is consumed, the authored structure appears and the link opens.
The main illustration shows completed routes; an open crate alongside a landing
is explanatory set dressing. The before/after study explains the actual states.

For simplicity, the crate is the site task rather than a new global resource.
Use existing wood/food costs if costs are needed; exact balancing is undecided.
Do not add separate rope, nails, ladder, bridge or parts inventories. Appearance
can vary naturally while every kit follows the same interaction rule.
Keep ordinary clearance jobs, such as opening a fence, where physically useful;
the kit unifies construction without making every object identical.

Validation requirements for a future playable implementation
- Reach the crate's work position from the open side before construction.
- Apply cost once, support queue cancellation, prevent duplicate assignments.
- Construction must open only the intended route, with no nearby bypass.
- Keep ground and raised links separate at screen-space crossings.
- Preserve a viable resource order for all mandatory kits; no kit needed to
  reach the only materials that can build that same kit.
- Give workers and rescued tourists working ascent, descent and return routes.
- Add foreground occlusion for underpasses and actor-above-deck rendering.
- Author production prop states together as an atlas where needed; this concept
  and its inset sketches are not production assets or a playable climb animation.

Visual review of generated revision
The southeastern sandy shortcut is replaced with a clearly supported timber
crossing, with red rails visible beneath it. The train rescue platform has a
wooden connection to that crossing. The upper catwalk gap is now decked, the
central entrance has boards, and the underpass has visible timber bracing.
The kit and its before/after example communicate a single construction job.
The resulting connected catwalk is more extensive than the minimum prototype;
start implementation with one short crossing and one ladder rather than assuming
that every illustrated span is required for the first playable test.

Comparable mechanics, checked 2026-10-19
GameHouse's Rescue Team 5 guide documents collecting resources, clearing
obstructions and spending wood to repair bridges. Its Roads of Rome: New
Generation guide documents workers constructing bridges and gathering materials.
These support the familiar gather/build/open-route loop, not a claim that either
game uses this exact shared construction-crate mechanic. The site-specific
crate-to-ladder/bridge/brace interaction is this prototype's design proposal.
